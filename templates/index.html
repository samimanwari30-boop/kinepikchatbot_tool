About KINEPIK Research Assistant

This is a conversational interface to KINEPIK, letting you ask natural-language questions about kinase activity, drug perturbations, signalling networks, and phosphorylation data — instead of querying the underlying API directly. Questions are routed to the right analysis, and answers are backed by real computed results, not generated from scratch.

What it can do

  • KSEA activity analysis — find the most activated or inhibited kinases under a given drug and cell line, or look up a single kinase directly
  • Comparisons — compare one kinase's activity across different drugs, across different cell lines, or as a heatmap across several kinases at once
  • Signalling networks — visualise a kinase's interaction network, or check whether one kinase targets a specific substrate
  • Phosphosite browsing — a searchable table of every phosphosite a kinase is known to target
  • Kinase information lookups — family, group, and classification data
  • Multi-step conversations — follow-up questions ("the top one", "its network") correctly resolve to whichever kinase was just discussed

Where the data comes from

All data is served from KINEPIK (kinepik.org), a database combining phosphoproteomics measurements with kinase-substrate interaction data — covering 504 kinases across 61 drug perturbations in 3 cell lines (MCF7, HL60, NTERA2). It draws on 13 published data sources, including:

  • LINCS P100 assay data — targeted mass-spectrometry profiling of around 1,000 phosphosites under chemical and CRISPR perturbations
  • KINOMEscan competition-binding assays — measuring drug-kinase binding across hundreds of kinases
  • Cell-line-specific experimental datasets for MCF7, HL60, and NTERA2, from thesis research on cell signalling network reasoning (Huebner, M.)
